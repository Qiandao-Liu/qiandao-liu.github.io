> 95% of robot control in this world is PID. The other 5% just doesn’t work.

<section class="project-story">
  <figure><img src="{{ '/images/mae4190/lab12/first_localization_pos.webp' | relative_url }}" alt="Initial robot localization on the known map" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Estimating the starting pose from a ToF scan.</figcaption></figure>
  <div><h2>From sensing to control</h2><p>A fast autonomous car built around onboard sensing and feedback control. A 360-degree time-of-flight scan and a Bayes filter establish the starting pose before the robot follows a sequence of waypoints.</p><p>Gyroscope-based PID control handles heading alignment in open space. Near the walls, front and side distance measurements guide steering, speed, and stopping.</p></div>
</section>

<section class="project-story project-story--reverse">
  <figure><img src="{{ '/images/mae4190/lab12/validation_in_sim_traj.webp' | relative_url }}" alt="Navigation trajectory validated in simulation" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Validating the route in simulation.</figcaption></figure>
  <div><h2>From simulation to the final run</h2><p>The final system combines timed motion through open areas with wall-following feedback. Motor control and safety stops run onboard; a laptop supplies segment parameters over Bluetooth.</p><p>The reports below document the progression through sensing, PID control, Kalman filtering, mapping, localization, and execution.</p></div>
</section>
